    Section Graceful degradation: three levels

    Level 1: Pair up
    🔗
    Use when: a minority of the room is offline or account-blocked.
    🔗
    Ask each stuck participant to pair with a neighbor who has a working editor. The partner drives; the stuck participant reads aloud the snippet to paste and decides what to customize. Both phases still work this way: the syllabus-building exercises are naturally collaborative, and the worksheet lesson gives the stuck participant something concrete to annotate on paper while watching.
    🔗
    Level 2: Instructor-driven demo with paper exercises
    🔗
    Use when: most of the room cannot edit, but the projector still works.
    🔗
    The facilitator drives a single pretext.plus project on the projector. Work through the syllabus-building exercises () live, one part at a time, with the room calling out the tags to use and the content to fill in. Distribute the printed participant handout so people can annotate each snippet by hand.
    🔗
    For the Worksheet Lesson phase (), distribute the printed product rule worksheet and ask participants to mark it up with the PreTeXt tags they’d use, referencing the cheatsheet. Work through the build on the projector while participants follow along on paper.
    🔗
    This level still preserves the core: participants see source, see preview, observe the Edit → Rebuild rhythm, and leave with a realistic mental model of the workflow.
    🔗
    Level 3: Fully offline read-and-compare
    🔗
    Use when: nothing online is usable, not even for the facilitator.
    🔗
    Distribute the prebuilt offline packet (see the next section). Walk through the “source on the left, output on the right” comparison sheets for the syllabus exercises, then hand out the printed product rule worksheet and ask participants to annotate the source in pencil and share with a neighbor. The session still ends on time and participants leave with a realistic mental model of the workflow.
