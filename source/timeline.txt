    <introduction>
      <p>
        Before you write any <pretext/> yourself, it helps to see where this is going. The key idea is that you describe <em>what your content is</em> — a definition, an example, an exercise, an interactive — and <pretext/> decides how it should look on the page, in a PDF, on a phone, or through a screen reader.
      </p>

      <p>
        The gallery below shows some of what's possible. You are <em>not</em> expected to build any of this today. Skim it, get a sense of the range, and come back to it later when you want ideas.

      <subsubsection xml:id="talk-demo-fill-in-blank">
        <title>✔ Fill-in-the-Blank Questions</title>

        <p>
          Accept any answer in a given set, or write your own check with a small snippet of JavaScript for richer feedback.
        </p>

        <exercise label="ex-demo-jscmp-primes">
        <title>Fill-In, Prime less then 20</title>
        <statement>
          <p>
            What is an example of a prime number less than 20?
            <fillin width="5" answer="13"/>
          </p>
        </statement>
        <evaluation>
          <evaluate>
            <test correct="yes">
              <jscmp><!-- test if number is in a list -->
                [2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19].includes(Number(ans))
              </jscmp>
              <feedback>
                <p>
                  Any number from the list <m>\{2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19\}</m> is a prime number less than 20.
                </p>
              </feedback>
            </test>
            <test>
              <jscmp><!-- find why it fails to be in list -->
                function(){
                  const val=Number(ans);
                  if (val &lt;= 0) { return "You need to give a positive integer." }
                  if (val &gt;= 20) { return "The integer must be less than 20." }
                  for (let i=2; i&lt;=3; i++) {
                  if (val % i == 0) { return `Your answer is composite since it is divisible by ${i}.` }
                }
                return false;
                }()
              </jscmp>
            </test>
          </evaluate>
        </evaluation>
        </exercise>
      </subsubsection>

    <introduction>

You'll make a free account, create a project, and get oriented to the editor. By the end of this phase you should have an empty project open and be ready to paste in content.

    <subsection xml:id="pp-create-account">
      <title>Create your account</title>

      <p>
        <ol>
          <li>
            <p>
              Go to <url href="https://pretext.plus"><c>pretext.plus</c></url> and click <term>Sign up</term>. Use an email you can check right now — there's a confirmation step.
            </p>
          </li>
          <li>
            <p>
              Confirm your email, then log in.
            </p>
          </li>
        </ol>
      </p>
    </subsection>

    <subsection xml:id="pp-first-project">
      <title>Create your first project</title>

      <p>
        <ol>
          <li>
            <p>
              Click <term>Projects</term> in the top nav, then the blue <term>New project</term> button.
            </p>
          </li>
          <li>
            <p>
              Give it a title — <c>My First Lesson</c> works fine — and click <term>Create</term>.
            </p>
          </li>
          <li>
            <p>
              Click the gray <term>Edit</term> button on your new project. The editor opens with <pretext/> source on the left and a live preview on the right.
            </p>
          </li>
        </ol>
      </p>
    </subsection>

    <subsection xml:id="pp-rebuild-rhythm">
      <title>Learn the rebuild rhythm</title>

      <p>
        The preview does <em>not</em> update automatically. After every edit, you need to rebuild.
      </p>

      <p>
        <ul>
          <li>Click the green <term>Rebuild</term> button at the top right of the preview, <em>or</em></li>
          <li>Press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd> (this also saves your work).</li>
        </ul>
      </p>

      <p>
        <alert>The rhythm is: Edit → Rebuild → look at the preview.</alert> You'll repeat that cycle dozens of times this afternoon. It becomes automatic within about ten minutes.
      </p>
    </subsection>

    <subsection xml:id="pp-orientation">
      <title>A quick orientation</title>

      <p>
        Before we start editing, take thirty seconds to notice:
      </p>

      <p>
        <ul>
          <li>The <em>source panel</em> on the left is where you type. It's <pretext/> XML — we'll get to what that means in the next section.</li>
          <li>The <em>preview panel</em> on the right is what your readers will see.</li>
          <li>There's a short starter document already loaded. Don't delete it — you'll paste new material <em>inside</em> it.</li>
        </ul>
      </p>

      <p>
        You're ready. On to the exercises.
      </p>
    </subsection>

              Click your project, then <term>Sharable link</term>.
            </p>
          </li>
          <li>
            <p>
              Copy the URL and give it to your neighbor — on paper, by email, or just turn your screen.
            </p>
          </li>
        </ol>
      </p>
    </paragraphs>

    <paragraphs>
      <title>What to look for</title>
      <p>
        <ul>
          <li>What's one thing their lesson does well?</li>
          <li>What's one thing you'd like to know how to do after seeing theirs?</li>
        </ul>
      </p>

      <p>
        That's it. This is a chance to see someone else's work, not a code review.
      </p>
    </paragraphs>
  </section>

  <!-- Appendix: Fallback plan                           -->
  <!-- ================================================= -->
  <section xml:id="sec-fallback-plan">
    <title>Appendix — Fallback Plan</title>

    <introduction>
      <p>
        <em>Facilitator reference.</em> The timeline above assumes the workshop runs as designed — everyone gets online, everyone's <c>pretext.plus</c> account works, and the editor behaves. This appendix is what to do if that isn't true.
      </p>

      <p>
        The goal of the fallback is not to replicate the full hands-on experience. It's to preserve the <em>core takeaway</em>: participants leave understanding what <pretext/> authoring looks and feels like, and with a concrete sense of what they'd write if they tried it themselves.
      </p>
    </introduction>

    <subsection xml:id="fb-triggers">
      <title>When to switch to fallback mode</title>

      <p>
        Switch — fully or partially — if any of these happen:
      </p>

      <p>
        <ul>
          <li>The venue Wi-Fi is down or throttled badly enough that <c>pretext.plus</c> won't load for most of the room.</li>
          <li>The <c>pretext.plus</c> service is slow, broken, or rejecting sign-ups.</li>
          <li>An institutional firewall is blocking account confirmation emails.</li>
          <li>More than about a quarter of the room can't get their editor working after ten minutes of troubleshooting.</li>
        </ul>
      </p>

      <p>
        Don't switch the whole room if only two or three people are stuck — pair them with neighbors who are working and keep going. Switch the whole room if the pattern is clearly environmental.
      </p>
    </subsection>

    <subsection xml:id="fb-levels">
      <title>Graceful degradation — three levels</title>

      <p>
        <dl>
          <li>
            <title>Level 1 — Pair up</title>
            <p>
              <em>Use when:</em> a minority of the room is offline or account-blocked.
            </p>
            <p>
              Ask each stuck participant to pair with a neighbor who has a working editor. The partner drives; the stuck participant reads aloud the snippet to paste and decides what to customize. Everyone still sees the Edit → Rebuild rhythm and leaves with a finished lesson associated with at least one of them.
            </p>
          </li>
          <li>
            <title>Level 2 — Instructor-driven demo with paper exercises</title>
            <p>
              <em>Use when:</em> most of the room cannot edit, but the projector still works.
            </p>
            <p>
              The facilitator drives a single <c>pretext.plus</c> project on the projector. Work through the five Guided Practice exercises live, one at a time, with the room calling out small edits. Distribute the printed participant handout so people can annotate each snippet by hand, writing in the changes they'd make. For <em>Build a Lesson</em>, hand out the two starter options on paper and ask participants to mark them up with their own topic — title changes, example swaps, reworded exercises. Collect a few at the end and project them as the Show and Tell.
            </p>
            <p>
              This level still preserves the core: participants see source, see preview, see a cross-reference resolve, and produce something concrete.
            </p>
          </li>
          <li>
            <title>Level 3 — Fully offline read-and-compare</title>
            <p>
              <em>Use when:</em> nothing online is usable, not even for the facilitator.
            </p>
            <p>
              Distribute the prebuilt offline packet (see the next subsection). Walk through the <q>source on the left, output on the right</q> comparison sheets, then have participants edit a printed starter lesson in pencil and share with a neighbor. The session still ends on time and participants leave with a realistic mental model of the workflow.
            </p>
          </li>
        </dl>
      </p>
    </subsection>

    <subsection xml:id="fb-offline-packet">
      <title>Offline packet to have on hand</title>

      <p>
        Bring the following in printed form (or on a local USB drive) before the session starts. This is cheap insurance.
      </p>

      <p>
        <ol>
          <li>
            <p>
              <em>Screenshots of the editor.</em> The <c>pretext.plus</c> landing page, an empty project, a project with source on the left and preview on the right, and the <term>Sharable link</term> dialog. Enough for someone who has never seen the tool to recognize what's happening.
            </p>
          </li>
          <li>
            <p>
              <em>Side-by-side <q>source vs. output</q> sheets</em> for each of the five Guided Practice exercises. Left column: the <pretext/> snippet. Right column: a screenshot of the rendered result.
            </p>
          </li>
          <li>
            <p>
              <em>Printed starter lessons.</em> Both Build-a-Lesson starters, printed with generous margins so participants can annotate in pencil.
            </p>
          </li>
          <li>
            <p>
              <em>A prebuilt sample HTML output</em> on a USB drive or local file that the facilitator can open without internet, so there's always something real to show on the projector.
            </p>
          </li>
          <li>
            <p>
              <em>This hub document as a PDF</em>, so participants can follow the timeline even without network access.
            </p>
          </li>
        </ol>
      </p>
    </subsection>

    <subsection xml:id="fb-completion-path">
      <title>A non-deployment completion path</title>

      <p>
        The default <q>done</q> signal is a sharable link. If sharing is broken, substitute any of these:
      </p>

      <p>
        <ul>
          <li>A screenshot of the participant's preview panel, sent to the facilitator or a neighbor.</li>
          <li>A printed marked-up starter lesson handed in at the end.</li>
          <li>A read-aloud of one thing they'd add if they had another hour — a lightweight oral <q>Show and Tell.</q></li>
        </ul>
      </p>

      <p>
        Whatever form it takes, each participant should leave having produced <em>something concrete</em> they could point at and say <q>I made that.</q> That's the contract.
      </p>
    </subsection>

    <subsection xml:id="fb-pacing">
      <title>Pacing adjustments</title>

      <p>
        If you've switched to Level 2 or 3, the timeline still holds roughly, but cut the following first:
      </p>

      <p>
        <ul>
          <li><em>Interactive Feature Gallery (4:10-4:20)</em> — compress to three favorites, shown on projector from the prebuilt HTML.</li>
          <li><em>Finished-early prompts in Guided Practice</em> — drop entirely.</li>
          <li><em>Show and Tell (5:40-5:50)</em> — shorten to one lesson or one collected paper starter, and give the extra minutes back to Build-a-Lesson.</li>
        </ul>
      </p>

      <p>
        Protect the Build-a-Lesson phase. That's where the core learning happens, and it's the last thing to cut.
      </p>
    </subsection>
  </section>
