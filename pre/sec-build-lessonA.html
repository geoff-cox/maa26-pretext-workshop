    Add the question below to the second page of your worksheet. It uses the same true/false pattern as the multiple-choice exercise you just built, with two small differences: the correct attribute moves onto the <statement> itself, and there is no <choices> block — just a single <feedback>.

    Checkpoint 10. True or false?

      If \(f\) and \(g\) are differentiable, then
      \begin{equation*} \frac{d}{dx}\bigl[f(x)\,g(x)\bigr] = f'(x)\,g'(x). \end{equation*}
      🔗

    • True.

    • The correct statement is \((fg)' = f'g + fg'\text{.}\) The discovery exercise on page 1 shows why: for \(f(x) = x^2\) and \(g(x) = x^3\text{,}\) the product \(f'g'\) gives \(6x^3\text{,}\) but \((fg)'\) gives \(5x^4\text{.}\)

    • False.

    • The correct statement is \((fg)' = f'g + fg'\text{.}\) The discovery exercise on page 1 shows why: for \(f(x) = x^2\) and \(g(x) = x^3\text{,}\) the product \(f'g'\) gives \(6x^3\text{,}\) but \((fg)'\) gives \(5x^4\text{.}\)
