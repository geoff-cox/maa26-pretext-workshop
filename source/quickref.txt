  <section xml:id="sec-create-account">
    <title>1. Create Your Account and First Project</title>

    <p>
      <em>We'll do this together.</em>
      <ol>
        <li>
          <p>
            Go to <c>pretext.plus</c> and click <term>Sign up</term>. Use an email you can check right now.
          </p>
        </li>

        <li>
          <p>
            After confirming your email, click <term>Projects</term>, then the blue <term>New project</term> button.
          </p>
        </li>

        <li>
          <p>
            Give your project a title (e.g. <c>My First Lesson</c>) and click <term>Create</term>.
          </p>
        </li>

        <li>
          <p>
            Click the gray <term>Edit</term> button. The editor opens with source on the left and a preview on the right.
          </p>
        </li>
      </ol>
    </p>
  </section>

  <section xml:id="sec-learn-rebuild-rhythm">
    <title>2. Learn the Rebuild Rhythm</title>

    <p>
      The preview does not update automatically. Click the green <term>Rebuild</term> button (top-right of the preview), or press <kbd>Ctrl</kbd>+<kbd>S</kbd>. This also saves your work.
    </p>

    <p>
      <alert>Edit → Rebuild → look at the preview.</alert>
    </p>
  </section>

  <section xml:id="sandbox-exercises">
    <title>2. While You Wait — Practice in the Sandbox</title>

    <introduction>
      <p>
        <em><c>pretext.plus/tryit</c> is a throwaway sandbox. You type PreTeXt source on
        the left, click <term>Rebuild</term>, and see the result on the right. Nothing you
        do here is saved — that is fine. Every exercise below is in this handout, so you
        can always paste it again.</em>
      </p>

      <p>
        <alert>How to work each exercise:</alert> find the closing <c>&lt;/section&gt;</c>
        tag near the bottom of the sandbox, and paste the snippet on a new line
        <em>just above it</em>. Then click <term>Rebuild</term> and look at the preview.
      </p>
    </introduction>

    <subsection xml:id="exercise-1">
      <title>Exercise 1 — A paragraph with inline math</title>
      <p>
        <em>Paste this inside the section and rebuild. Notice how inline math
        flows with the text.</em>
      </p>
      <program language="xml">
        <code>
<![CDATA[<p>The Pythagorean theorem states that <m>a^2 + b^2 = c^2</m>,
where <m>c</m> is the length of the hypotenuse.</p>]]>
        </code>
      </program>
    </subsection>

    <subsection xml:id="exercise-2">
      <title>Exercise 2 — A definition</title>
      <p>
        <em>Add a formal definition. The <c>xml:id</c> is a label we will use in Exercise 5.</em>
      </p>
      <program language="xml">
        <code>
<![CDATA[<definition xml:id="def-right-triangle">
<statement>
  <p>A <term>right triangle</term> is a triangle
  with one angle measuring exactly 90 degrees.</p>
</statement>
</definition>]]>
        </code>
      </program>
    </subsection>

    <subsection xml:id="exercise-3">
      <title>Exercise 3 — An example</title>
      <p>
        <em>Examples get numbered automatically. Try changing the title after you rebuild.</em>

<statement>
  <p>Show that a triangle with sides 3, 4, and 5

</solution>

  <section xml:id="sec-your-target">
    <title>4. Your Target — A Sample Lesson</title>

    <p>
      <em>After the break, build a short lesson on a topic you actually teach, shaped like the example below.</em>
    </p>

    <p>
      <alert>Safety net:</alert> if stuck, paste the block below and change the topic.
    </p>

    <program language="xml">
      <code><![CDATA[
      <title>The Product and Quotient Rules</title>

      <p>
        When a function is built by multiplying or dividing
        two simpler functions, we cannot just multiply or
        divide their derivatives.
      </p>

      <definition xml:id="def-product-rule">
        <statement>
          <p>
            The <term>product rule</term> states that if
            <m>f</m> and <m>g</m> are differentiable, then
            <me>
              \frac{d}{dx}\bigl[f(x)\,g(x)\bigr]
              = f'(x)\,g(x) + f(x)\,g'(x).
            </me>
          </p>
        </statement>
      </definition>

      <example>
        <title>Applying the product rule</title>

        <statement>
          <p>
            Find the derivative of <m>h(x) = x^2 \sin(x)</m>.
          </p>
        </statement>

        <solution>
          <p>
            Let <m>f(x) = x^2</m> and <m>g(x) = \sin(x)</m>.
            Then
            <me>
              h'(x) = 2x \sin(x) + x^2 \cos(x).
            </me>
          </p>
        </solution>
      </example>

      <exercise>
        <statement>
          <p>
            Use the product rule
            (see <xref ref="def-product-rule"/>) to find
            the derivative of <m>p(x) = x^3 e^x</m>.
          </p>
        </statement>
      </exercise>
      ]]></code>
    </program>
  </section>
