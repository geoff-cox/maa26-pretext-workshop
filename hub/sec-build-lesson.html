    Section 5:10-5:35 Worksheet Lesson

    Choose your Own Adventure.

    This is the main event. You have about twenty-five minutes to build a short lesson using the structures you met in Guided Practice. Pick an approach below based on how confident you’re feeling and what you want to work on.
    🔗
    Path A — Use a starter
    🔗
    Pick one of the two starter lessons below, paste it into your project, rebuild, and then make it yours — change the topic, swap the example, rewrite the exercise. The scaffolding is already there; you’re customizing.
    🔗
    Best if you want a guaranteed win or you aren’t sure what to write about.
    🔗
    Path B — Write your own
    🔗
    Start from a topic you actually teach and build a short lesson from scratch using the patterns from Guided Practice. Aim for: one paragraph of motivation, one definition, one worked example, one exercise, and one cross-reference.
    🔗
    Best if you already have something in mind and want to try the real thing.
    🔗
    🔗
    Either path is a success. Nobody is getting graded on ambition.
    🔗

    Subsection Starter option 1 — Right triangles

    A geometry lesson with a definition, a named example, and an exercise that references the definition by cross-reference.
    🔗

    Subsubsection Right Triangles

    This short lesson introduces right triangles and the Pythagorean theorem.
    🔗
    Definition 10.
    A right triangle is a triangle with one angle measuring exactly 90 degrees.
    🔗
    🔗
    The Pythagorean theorem states that \(a^2 + b^2 = c^2\text{,}\) where \(c\) is the length of the hypotenuse.
    🔗
    Example 11. A 3-4-5 triangle.
    Show that a triangle with sides 3, 4, and 5 is a right triangle.
    🔗
    Solution.
    Check: \(3^2 + 4^2 = 9 + 16 = 25 = 5^2\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Checkpoint 12.
    Find the length of the hypotenuse of a right triangle with legs of length 5 and 12.
    🔗
    🔗
    For a reminder of the terminology, see Definition 10.
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection Starter option 2 — The product rule

    A calculus lesson with a display-math definition, a worked example that uses the definition, and an exercise that sends students back to the definition via cross-reference.
    🔗
    <title>The Product and Quotient Rules</title>

    <p>
    When a function is built by multiplying or dividing
    two simpler functions, we cannot just multiply or
    divide their derivatives.
    </p>

    <definition xml:id="def-product-rule">
    <statement>
    <p>
      The <term>product rule</term> states that if
      <m>f</m> and <m>g</m> are differentiable, then
      <me>
        \frac{d}{dx}\bigl[f(x)\,g(x)\bigr]
        = f'(x)\,g(x) + f(x)\,g'(x).
      </me>
    </p>
    </statement>
    </definition>

    <example>
    <title>Applying the product rule</title>
    <statement>
    <p>
      Find the derivative of <m>h(x) = x^2 \sin(x)</m>.
    </p>
    </statement>
    <solution>
    <p>
      Let <m>f(x) = x^2</m> and <m>g(x) = \sin(x)</m>.
      Then
      <me>
        h'(x) = 2x \sin(x) + x^2 \cos(x).
      </me>
    </p>
    </solution>
    </example>

    <exercise>
    <statement>
    <p>
      Use the product rule
      (see <xref ref="def-product-rule"/>) to find
      the derivative of <m>p(x) = x^3 e^x</m>.
    </p>
    </statement>
    </exercise>
    🔗

    Milestones.

    Whichever path you pick, aim for these milestones. Don’t worry about hitting them in order.
    🔗
    1. A title and a paragraph. The lesson has a topic and a short lead-in.
      🔗
      🔗
    2. A definition with an xml:id. Your reader has a formal statement of the main idea.
      🔗
      🔗
    3. A worked example. You show the reader how the idea gets used.
      🔗
      🔗
    4. An exercise. The reader gets a chance to practice.
      🔗
      🔗
    5. A cross-reference. Somewhere in the lesson, an <xref/> links back to your definition or example.
      🔗
      🔗
    🔗
    Hitting all five in twenty-five minutes is very doable. Hitting three is also fine. You’re building a habit, not a textbook.
    🔗

    If you get stuck.

    Red error in the preview
    🔗
    Almost always a missing or mismatched tag. The error message usually has a line number. If you can’t find it, paste the starter again and re-edit more carefully.
    🔗
    Preview didn’t update
    🔗
    Click Rebuild or press Ctrl+S. The preview doesn’t refresh on its own.
    🔗
    Lost your work
    🔗
    Click Projects in the top nav. It’s saved.
    🔗
    Something weirder
    🔗
    Raise your hand. Or ask the person next to you first — chances are they’ve just hit the same thing.
